I Tested 50+ Car Accessories Over 18 Months. These Are the Only 7 Worth Your Money.

By CarGadgetFinds Editorial Team · 8 min read · Real testing, no sponsored content

The car accessory market is a $40 billion industry — and 90% of it is junk. Over the past 18 months, our team bought, installed, and tested 50+ products across every category: from $15 phone mounts to $300 radar detectors. We left them in heat, rain, and snow. We installed them, removed them, and installed them again. Here's the brutally honest shortlist — the seven products that earned a permanent spot in our vehicles, and why everything else got returned or thrown away.

#1: A Portable Jump Starter (Not Jumper Cables)

Why it's worth it: A dead battery always happens at the worst time — in a parking garage at night, in the rain, when you're already late. Jumper cables require flagging down a stranger and positioning two cars nose-to-nose. A portable jump starter (about the size of a water bottle) starts your car in 30 seconds, by yourself, anywhere. The one we tested started a V6 truck 14 times on one charge. It also charges your phone and has a flashlight. We've used ours 6 times in the past year — for ourselves and stranded strangers. Cost: $50–$80. Saved us $120 in tow truck fees the first time we used it.

What to avoid: The $25 no-name units on Amazon. We tested three — two couldn't jump-start even a 4-cylinder engine, and one died after 3 months. Stick to NOCO, Schumacher, or AstroAI.

#2: A Tire Inflator With a Digital Gauge

Why it's worth it: Proper tire pressure improves gas mileage by 3–5%, extends tire life by 20%, and prevents blowouts. Yet most people never check their tires until the TPMS light comes on — which means they've been driving underinflated for months. A 12V tire inflator plugs into your cigarette lighter, inflates a tire from flat to full in 3–5 minutes, and shuts off automatically at your target pressure. We use ours monthly — before road trips, after temperature swings, and when hauling heavy loads. Cost: $30–$45. Pays for itself in gas savings and avoided tire replacements.

What to avoid: The $15 units with analog gauges — they're inaccurate by 5+ PSI, which is dangerous. Always get digital. And skip the cordless battery ones — they're never charged when you need them. The 12V plug-in type is always ready.

#3: A Dash Cam (Obviously — But Get the Right One)

Why it's worth it: I won't belabor this one — we have an entire guide. But here's the short version: a dash cam is the only product on this list that can single-handedly save you $5,000+ in a single accident. False insurance claims, hit-and-runs, brake-checking scammers — all solved with video. Cost: $60–$150. ROI: incalculable.

What to avoid: Sub-$50 cameras (they fail), and 4K if you're on a budget — 2K is the sweet spot. Read the 7 mistakes to avoid →

#4: All-Weather Floor Mats (Not the Cheap Rubber Kind)

Why it's worth it: Your carpet is one of the first things potential buyers notice — and stains, salt, and pet hair destroy it permanently. Cheap $20 rubber mats slide around, curl at the edges, and don't cover the footwell. Custom-fit all-weather mats (WeatherTech, Husky, or the much cheaper Lasfit/Kust alternatives) lock into place, contain liquid spills, and hose clean in 2 minutes. We tested a $40 set of Lasfit mats against $200 WeatherTech — the difference was negligible for 90% of drivers. Cost: $40–$120. Protects $1,000+ in resale value.

What to avoid: Universal "trim-to-fit" mats — they never fit right and slide under the pedals (dangerous!). Always get vehicle-specific.

#5: A Backup Camera (If Your Car Doesn't Have One)

Why it's worth it: If your car was built before 2018, it doesn't have a factory backup camera. Backup cameras reduce backing accidents by 94%. For trucks, SUVs, and anyone with kids in the neighborhood, it's not optional. A $40 wireless model installs in 30 minutes. We installed one on a 2012 pickup and the difference in confidence while parking was night and day. Cost: $40–$100. Prevents even one fender bender = $500+ saved.

What to avoid: Analog wireless (signal drops). Get digital wireless or wired. See our tested picks →

#6: A Quality Windshield Sun Shade

Why it's worth it: This is the most boring, unsexy accessory on the list — and the one we use every single day in summer. A $25 folding sun shade keeps your interior 30–40°F cooler, prevents dash cracking and fading, and means you don't burn your hands on the steering wheel. We tested cheap $10 shades (they sag, don't cover the whole windshield) vs. $25–$35 custom-fit ones (perfect coverage, rigid, last 5+ years). Cost: $20–$35. Extends interior life by years.

What to avoid: The cheap accordion-style ones that fall down. Get a rigid folding or roll-up type that fits your windshield exactly.

#7: A Basic Emergency Roadside Kit

Why it's worth it: You hope to never use it. But when you need it — stranded on the highway at night with a flat tire in the rain — a reflective triangle, flashlight, and first aid kit are worth their weight in gold. A $30 pre-assembled kit includes everything you need. We keep one in every vehicle. Cost: $25–$40. Peace of mind: priceless.

What to avoid: Kits with 100+ pieces where 90 of them are band-aids. Look for: reflective triangle, jumper cables (or rely on your jump starter), flashlight with extra batteries, first aid basics, duct tape, and a warm blanket.

What We Tested and Returned (Save Your Money)

  • Radar detectors: $300+ and Waze is free and more accurate. Returned.
  • Decorative chrome trim / license plate frames: Look cheap after 6 months, some are illegal. Returned.
  • Car air fresheners (plug-in type): Overpowering, leave residue. A $5 vent clip works better.
  • Seat covers (universal): Never fit right, interfere with side airbags. If you need them, get custom-fit — otherwise skip.
  • Steering wheel covers: Slippery when wet, look tacky. If your wheel is worn, just replace it — or use it as an excuse to upgrade to a leather wheel.
